- **Perché interessa al pubblico:** Infrastructure investor, lender, professionisti di project e leveraged finance.
- **Messaggio principale:** Il mercato non sta abbandonando i data center: sta separando la capacità con contratto da quella speculativa.
- **Tesi:** Valutazioni in calo e investimenti in crescita non sono una contraddizione se si guarda la qualità del contratto e del partner, non il tema AI.
- **Domanda finale:** If you were lending to a data centre today, would you rather have a 15-year hyperscaler lease or a strategic co-owner?

## Post completo

Data centre stocks are falling. Data centre owners are still committing billions. Who is wrong?

This weekend's headlines, side by side:
- FT: data centre construction stocks have lost $4.2tn as the AI trade unravels.
- FT: Samsung, after winning in AI chips, wants to build data centres.
- Nikkei Asia: Digital Realty plans to spend another $10bn on data centres in Japan, according to its CEO.

I read these from headlines, so the numbers need checking. The pattern is still worth a question.

Equity is repricing the whole theme. Owners with long contracts and strong partners keep building. Digital Realty works in Japan through a 50/50 joint venture with Mitsubishi. Samsung SDS broke ground on a 60MW AI data centre in Gumi on 2 November.

My working thesis: the market is not exiting data centres. It is splitting them into two asset classes.

One is contracted capacity: long leases, investment-grade tenants, strategic co-owners. It looks like infrastructure and should be financed like infrastructure.

The other is speculative capacity: no anchor tenant, short contracts, sponsors that need the IPO to repay the debt. That looks like venture risk with a construction budget.

[Spazio per la tua esperienza: es. come valuti il rischio di controparte in un contratto di lungo periodo nelle infrastrutture in fibra, senza dettagli riservati.]

If you were lending to a data centre today, would you rather have a 15-year hyperscaler lease or a strategic co-owner?
