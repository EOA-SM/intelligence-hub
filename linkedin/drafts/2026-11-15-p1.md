# Oil fell all week. Then three tankers caught fire in Hormuz on Saturday.

- **Argomento:** Energy shock as a credit variable
- **Perché interessa al pubblico:** CFO, lender e infrastructure/leveraged finance con costi energetici rilevanti.
- **Messaggio principale:** Lo shock energetico non è solo un tema di ricavi: passa nei tassi e nei covenant nello stesso momento.
- **Tesi:** Nei modelli il prezzo dell'energia e il tasso vengono stressati separatamente; uno shock d'offerta li muove insieme, e il caso combinato è quello che rompe i covenant.
- **Domanda finale:** In your models, do energy prices and rates move together in the downside case, or one at a time?
- **Fonte:** Reuters, 14/11/2026 — https://www.yahoo.com/news/world/articles/irans-navy-says-three-tankers-225347151.html

## Post completo

Oil fell all week. Then three tankers caught fire in Hormuz on Saturday.

Iran's navy and Revolutionary Guards said the ships struck mines in the strait. Reuters could not verify the claims, and no vessel names were given. Overnight, Bahrain issued its first threat alerts since September.

The facts on the ground are still unclear. The modelling lesson is not.

Most downside cases I see shock one thing at a time: energy costs up in one scenario, rates up in another. A supply shock does both at once. Energy prices rise, inflation expectations follow, and a Fed already debating hikes has one less reason to wait. The 10-year Treasury was above 5% this week.

For an infrastructure or energy-intensive borrower, that combined case is where covenant headroom disappears:

1. Opex rises before any indexation in the contracts kicks in.
2. The floating portion of the debt reprices at the same time.
3. The hedge, if it rolls in the next 12 months, rolls at a worse rate.

[Spazio per la tua esperienza: es. come avete trattato costo dell'energia e tassi nello stesso scenario nel progetto rinnovabile da 80 MW o nel rifinanziamento da 120 milioni, senza dettagli riservati.]

None of this says oil will spike on Monday. It says a model that only works if energy and rates do not move together is a base case, not a stress case.

In your models, do energy prices and rates move together in the downside case, or one at a time?
