## Post completo

Japan's economy shrank last quarter. On the same day, a Bank of Japan board member asked for faster rate hikes.

The facts: Q3 GDP fell 0.1% annualised against expectations of growth, with consumption down 0.3%. Hours later, Hajime Takata said the BoJ should move quickly toward a 'good' level near the middle of its staff's neutral range, estimated at 1.1% to 2.5%. The policy rate is 1.25%. Last month he proposed 1.5% and was outvoted.

Takata is a minority voice. But it is worth running the scenario where he wins.

If the policy rate drifts toward 1.75-2% in 2027, three things change for cross-border deals:

1. The yen is no longer the obvious cheap funding currency. Carry structures that assumed near-zero yen rates need a new base case.
2. Hedging costs move. A euro buyer hedging yen cash flows, or a Japanese buyer hedging euro debt, faces a different rate gap than the one in last year's model.
3. Japanese acquirers abroad, a steady source of bids in European mid-market processes, see their own cost of capital rise.
