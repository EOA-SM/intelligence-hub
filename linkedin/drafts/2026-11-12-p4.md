# The next wave of luxury deals may be written in wills, not in strategy decks.

- **Argomento:** Succession as the pipeline of luxury M&A and ECM
- **Perché interessa al pubblico:** Advisor M&A ed ECM, family office, PE consumer, CFO di aziende del lusso a controllo familiare.
- **Messaggio principale:** Le successioni nelle famiglie del lusso creano operazioni con scadenze fisse, indipendenti dal ciclo.
- **Tesi:** Quando la vendita è imposta da un testamento o da un passaggio generazionale, il calendario non lo decide il mercato. Con multipli compressi, il valore lo fa la struttura: minoranze, IPO a tappe, partner industriali.
- **Domanda finale:** If you had to sell 15% of a family-controlled luxury house by a fixed date, in a weak market: strategic buyer, financial investor, or IPO?

## Post completo

The next wave of luxury deals may be written in wills, not in strategy decks.

The Armani case is the clearest example. According to Reuters, the will requires the heirs to sell about 15% by March 2027 and points to an IPO within three to eight years. Bankers put the group's value at €5–7bn. L'Oréal and EssilorLuxottica are seen as the most engaged suitors, while the heirs reportedly prefer a listing.

At the other end of the scale, Style Capital has just taken control of N°21 after its industrial partner exited. Different size, same pattern: ownership changes driven by the shareholders' situation, not by the market cycle.

A scenario for the next 24 months:
→ More minority sales with a path to IPO, because sellers cannot wait for multiples to recover.
→ Strategic buyers taking small stakes with options, rather than full control.
→ Financing structures that bridge the gap: preferred equity, holdco debt, earn-outs linked to brand performance.

The risk is obvious: a fixed deadline in a soft market weakens the seller's hand. The structure becomes the main lever on value.
