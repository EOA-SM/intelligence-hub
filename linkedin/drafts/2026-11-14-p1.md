# The Fed just put a number on the AI buildout's hidden leverage. It is not the debt.

- **Argomento:** Off-balance-sheet leverage in the AI buildout (Fed Financial Stability Report)
- **Perché interessa al pubblico:** Lender, private credit e leveraged finance; chi struttura project finance su infrastrutture digitali.
- **Messaggio principale:** La vera leva del ciclo AI è nei contratti, non nei bilanci.
- **Tesi:** Il rapporto della Fed misura per la prima volta gli impegni fuori bilancio degli hyperscaler: sono quasi il triplo del debito. Chi presta ai veicoli più piccoli sta prestando, in realtà, contro la qualità di quei contratti.
- **Domanda finale:** When you lend to a project backed by a big-tech lease, do you underwrite the project or the contract?
- **Fonte:** Federal Reserve Board, Financial Stability Report (13/11/2026) — https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/financial-stability-report-20261113.pdf

## Post completo

The Fed just put a number on the AI buildout's hidden leverage. It is not the debt.

In its November Financial Stability Report, the Fed estimates that debt linked to advanced computing passed $680bn of issuance by October 2026, five times the 2023 level. Public debt outstanding is above $1.1tn.

The bigger figure sits off balance sheet. For seven large tech firms, leases not yet started exceed $1tn, and take-or-pay contracts and residual value guarantees exceed $2tn (undiscounted).

My reading: the risk is moving from strong balance sheets to weaker vehicles through contracts. The subordinated tranches of those SPEs sit with high-yield funds, life insurers and BDCs.

For anyone who structures or lends to infrastructure, this changes the credit question:
→ The asset matters less than the lease behind it.
→ Termination rights and step-down clauses are the real collateral.
→ A residual value guarantee is only as good as the guarantor's next five years.
