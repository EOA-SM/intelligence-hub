# AI compute is now being financed like infrastructure. Is it infrastructure?

- **Argomento:** Compute-backed SPV and residual value risk
- **Perché interessa al pubblico:** Infrastructure e project finance, private credit, banche, tech investor.
- **Messaggio principale:** Gli strumenti di project finance funzionano sul compute solo se qualcuno prezza correttamente il valore residuo di un asset che invecchia in pochi anni.
- **Tesi:** Il deal Broadcom/Anthropic da 63 miliardi usa lo schema delle infrastrutture: SPV, leasing, first e second lien, supporto sul valore residuo. La differenza è la vita utile: 30 anni per la fibra, pochi anni per un acceleratore. Il rischio vero è nella garanzia sul valore residuo.
- **Domanda finale:** Who should carry residual value risk on AI hardware: the vendor, the lender, or the user?

## Post completo

AI compute is now being financed like infrastructure. Is it infrastructure?

Last month Bank of America, Citi, Morgan Stanley and Blackstone put together $63bn for Broadcom AI chips and networking gear destined for Anthropic. The structure looks very familiar to anyone in infrastructure finance:
• An SPV buys the equipment and leases it to the user.
• The banks arrange the first lien (about $43.5bn, according to two sources); Blackstone provides the whole second lien.
• Broadcom provides residual value support equal to the first-lien amount.

I work on fibre networks, where the same tools apply. But there is one big difference: useful life. A fibre network is financed over decades, while an accelerator can be overtaken by the next generation in a few years.

That moves the key risk from the user's credit to the residual value guarantee. If the hardware is worth less than expected when the lease ends, someone absorbs the gap, and here that someone is largely the vendor.

Reuters reported this week that banks and credit investors are looking harder at AI-related debt after the market sell-off. Moody's has warned that risk rises if many deals like this are done in a short period.

I don't think the structure is wrong. I think the price of the residual value guarantee is the number to watch, more than the headline size.

Who should carry residual value risk on AI hardware: the vendor, the lender, or the user?
