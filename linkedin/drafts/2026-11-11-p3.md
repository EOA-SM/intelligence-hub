# Chinese demand is soft. Luxury groups are answering by buying their suppliers.

- **Argomento:** Vertical integration as the next luxury M&A cycle
- **Perché interessa al pubblico:** Operatori del lusso, M&A advisor, PE con focus consumer, imprenditori della filiera italiana.
- **Messaggio principale:** Il prossimo ciclo di M&A nel lusso sarà fatto di piccoli deal a monte, non di grandi fusioni tra marchi.
- **Tesi:** Con la domanda cinese debole e valutazioni compresse, i gruppi investono dove controllano il risultato: costi, qualità e capacità produttiva. Per le PMI della filiera italiana si aprono exit e finanziamenti, ma anche una perdita di indipendenza.
- **Domanda finale:** If you run a supplier in the luxury chain, would you rather sell a majority to one maison or stay independent and serve five?

## Post completo

Chinese demand is soft. Luxury groups are answering by buying their suppliers.

This year's Singles' Day is flat, and big-ticket goods are among the weakest categories, according to AP. China is roughly a quarter of sales for the large groups, and brokers are cutting targets on that basis.

At the same time, in Italy:
• Louis Vuitton has reportedly moved from 40% to 60% of Mab manifatture, an embroidery specialist in Abruzzo.
• Chanel has a project of around €100m in the Pistoia area.
• LVMH invests around €500m a year in Italy.
(Source: MF-Milano Finanza)

My read: the next M&A cycle in luxury won't be about brand-on-brand mergers. It will be dozens of small deals upstream, where the buyer is paying for know-how, capacity and control over quality and cost. When you can't control demand, you control the things you can.

This has consequences for the Italian supply chain:
→ Founders get an exit that didn't exist ten years ago, often at better multiples than a financial buyer would pay.
→ They also lose the ability to work for competing maisons, which is how many of them grew.
→ Lenders need to price customer concentration, not just margins.

[Spazio per la tua esperienza: il lavoro sul business plan per un produttore del lusso e la riduzione dei costi di produzione di circa il 20%, senza dati riservati.]
