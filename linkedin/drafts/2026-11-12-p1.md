# Nvidia's board met last night to discuss removing its founder. Most deal models have no line for that.

- **Argomento:** Key-person risk in founder-led companies
- **Perché interessa al pubblico:** M&A e leveraged finance, lender, PE: chi valuta o finanzia società guidate dal fondatore.
- **Messaggio principale:** Il key-person risk è un rischio di credito e di valutazione, non una nota a margine.
- **Tesi:** Nei modelli si stressano ricavi, margini e tassi, ma raramente la governance. Il caso Nvidia mostra quanto valore può muoversi in un'ora quando il rischio è una persona.
- **Domanda finale:** In your last deal, was key-person risk in the model, in the documentation, or nowhere?
- **Fonte:** CNBC, CNN, NBC News, Reuters via France 24 (11–12/11/2026) — https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/11/nvidia-jensen-huang-affair-employee.html

## Post completo

Nvidia's board met last night to discuss removing its founder. Most deal models have no line for that.

The facts so far: according to the Wall Street Journal and others, Nvidia's board met on Wednesday evening to decide on Jensen Huang's future, after reports of two personal relationships. The shares fell 7–9% after hours. On a company worth about $4.2 trillion, that is close to $400 billion. No decision had been announced when Asia opened.

Whatever the outcome, the case is a useful lesson for anyone who prices or lends to founder-led businesses.

We stress revenue, margins, CapEx and, lately, rates. We rarely stress governance. Yet in founder-led companies the founder is often the strategy, the main customer relationship and the reason lenders are comfortable.

Three places where this should show up:
→ Valuation: a discount, or at least a scenario, for an unplanned succession.
→ Documentation: key-person and change-of-management clauses that are tested, not boilerplate.
→ Counterparties: suppliers and customers whose own plans depend on that one company.
