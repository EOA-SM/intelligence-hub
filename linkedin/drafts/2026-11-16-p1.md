# Blackstone offered a 36% premium. The board said no, and refused due diligence.

- **Argomento:** Take-private pricing at trough earnings (IDP–Blackstone)
- **Perché interessa al pubblico:** PE, leveraged finance e M&A: chi prezza take-private su società in rilancio.
- **Messaggio principale:** Il divario di prezzo nei take-private nasce dalla base di utili usata: minimi attuali contro ripresa attesa.
- **Tesi:** Un premio alto sul prezzo di borsa può essere basso rispetto agli utili normalizzati; negare la due diligence obbliga lo sponsor e i suoi finanziatori a lavorare sugli utili minimi.
- **Domanda finale:** When you value a target whose earnings have just fallen, do you lend on trough EBITDA or on the recovery case?

## Post completo

Blackstone offered a 36% premium. The board said no, and refused due diligence.

On Monday, IDP Education rejected Blackstone's third proposal: A$3.00 per share in cash, about A$834m, 20% above the A$2.50 bid it turned down in September. The board called it opportunistic and said it substantially undervalued the business. IDP's annual net profit fell almost 75% in August.

The lesson is about where the price gap comes from.

A sponsor prices a take-private on today's earnings and the leverage they can carry. A board in a turnaround prices it on the earnings it expects to recover. When profit has just collapsed, those two numbers are furthest apart, so a headline premium can still look cheap to the seller.

Refusing due diligence is the sharpest tool the board has. Without data, the bidder cannot underwrite the recovery case, and the lenders cannot size the debt on anything but trough earnings.

[Spazio per la tua esperienza: es. come la scelta tra EBITDA corrente e normalizzato ha cambiato il debito sostenibile nella valutazione di un target, senza dettagli riservati.]

Three options now sit with Blackstone: pay for the recovery, go around the board to shareholders, or walk away. Each one says something about how sponsors price turnarounds in 2026.

When you value a target whose earnings have just fallen, do you lend on trough EBITDA or on the recovery case?
